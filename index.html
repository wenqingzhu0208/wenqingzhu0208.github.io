About

I study how a virus's genome behaves in the first minutes after it enters a bacterial cell, one molecule at a time.

I'm a Ph.D. candidate at UIUC, trained in chemistry and physics and now working in biophysics. My research combines super-resolution fluorescence microscopy, click-chemistry labeling, and computational modeling to follow bacteriophage infection inside E. coli. Using 3D MINFLUX nanoscopy with EdU-labeled phage DNA, I measure the shape and organization of single injected genomes at nanometer resolution, and use polymer physics to interpret what we see.
